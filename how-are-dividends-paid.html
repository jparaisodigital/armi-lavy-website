DIVIDEND INVESTING BASICS NOVEMBER 10, 2026 6 MIN READ

How are dividends paid?

Dividends are payments a company or fund may distribute to eligible shareholders. They are commonly paid in cash, although some investors choose to automatically reinvest the payment into additional shares.

The exact amount, schedule, and payment method depend on the company, fund, and brokerage account.

If you're new to dividend investing, you can also explore the dividend investing glossary for more beginner-friendly terms.

Where does a dividend payment go?

For most investors, a cash dividend is deposited directly into the brokerage account that holds the shares.

Once the payment arrives, the cash may remain in the account, be withdrawn, or be used to purchase other investments.

The investor usually does not need to contact the company directly. The payment is typically processed through the brokerage or account provider.

How often are dividends paid?

There is no single payment schedule used by every company or fund.

Many U.S. companies pay dividends quarterly, while others may pay monthly, semiannually, annually, or on another schedule.

Some companies do not pay dividends at all, and dividend payments can be reduced or stopped.

What is dividend reinvestment?

Instead of receiving a dividend as cash, an investor may choose to reinvest it into additional shares of the same investment.

Many brokerage accounts offer a dividend reinvestment feature, sometimes called a DRIP.

Reinvestment can increase the number of shares owned over time, but it does not remove investment risk or guarantee future returns.

How do you become eligible for a dividend?

Receiving an upcoming dividend depends on when the shares are purchased relative to the ex-dividend date.

In general, an investor who buys shares on or after the ex-dividend date will not receive that upcoming payment.

To learn more about the timing, read What Is the Ex-Dividend Date? .

How much dividend income will you receive?

The amount depends on the dividend declared per share and the number of eligible shares owned.

For example, if a company pays a .50 dividend per share and an investor owns 100 eligible shares, the gross dividend payment would be $50.

Taxes, account type, withholding rules, and other factors may affect the amount ultimately available to the investor.

How does dividend yield fit in?

Dividend yield is different from the actual payment process. It is a percentage that compares a company's annual dividend with its current share price.

You can learn more in What Is Dividend Yield? .

Investors may also review the dividend payout ratio when considering how much of a company's earnings are being paid as dividends.

What about dividend ETFs?

Dividend-focused ETFs can also make distributions to shareholders. These payments may come from dividends received from the securities held inside the fund.

The schedule and amount can vary by ETF, and distributions are not guaranteed.

You can explore the free dividend and ETF tools for educational comparisons and estimates.

Key takeaway

Dividends are commonly paid as cash into an investor's brokerage account, although the payment may also be reinvested into additional shares.

The amount and timing depend on the investment, the number of eligible shares owned, and the company's or fund's distribution schedule.
