What is a dividend payout ratio?

Dividend payout ratio shows how much of a company's earnings are being paid to shareholders as dividends.

It can help investors think about whether a dividend looks sustainable, but it should be considered alongside the company's cash flow, debt, industry, and overall financial health.

How is payout ratio calculated?

A common formula is:

Dividend Payout Ratio = Dividends Per Share ÷ Earnings Per Share × 100

For example, suppose a company earns $5 per share and pays $2 per share in annual dividends.

$2 ÷ $5 = 0.40, or a 40% payout ratio.

In simple terms, that means 40% of earnings are being distributed as dividends while the remaining earnings may be retained by the company.

What does a high payout ratio mean?

A high payout ratio means a company is distributing a large portion of its earnings to shareholders.

That is not automatically bad. Different industries and mature businesses can have very different payout patterns.

However, an unusually high payout ratio may leave less room for reinvestment, debt reduction, or future dividend increases.

A payout ratio above 100% means the company is paying out more in dividends than it is currently earning, which may be difficult to sustain indefinitely.

Is a low payout ratio always better?

Not necessarily.

A lower payout ratio can mean the company is retaining more earnings for growth, debt repayment, or future dividend increases.

But a low payout ratio alone does not tell you whether the business is financially strong or whether the dividend will grow.

Payout ratio vs. dividend yield

Dividend yield tells you how much dividend income a stock pays relative to its share price.

Payout ratio tells you how much of the company's earnings are being used to support that dividend.

Used together, the two metrics can provide more context than either one alone.

What else should you review?

When evaluating dividend sustainability, investors may also review:

  • Free cash flow
  • Earnings consistency
  • Debt levels
  • Dividend history
  • Industry conditions
  • Company growth plans

Payout ratio is useful, but it is only one part of a broader financial picture.
