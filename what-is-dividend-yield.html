What is dividend yield?

Dividend yield is a percentage that compares a company's annual dividend payments with its current share price.

It can help you understand how much dividend income a stock may provide relative to the amount invested, but it should not be used by itself to decide whether an investment is attractive.

If you're new to investing terminology, you can also explore the dividend investing glossary.

How is dividend yield calculated?

The basic formula is:

Dividend Yield = Annual Dividend Per Share ÷ Share Price × 100

For example, suppose a company pays $2.00 per share in annual dividends and its stock trades at $50.

$2 ÷ $50 = 0.04, or a 4% dividend yield.

That percentage describes the dividend income relative to the current share price. It does not include gains or losses caused by changes in the stock price.

Is a higher dividend yield always better?

No. A high dividend yield can look attractive, but the reason behind the high yield matters.

Dividend yield can rise simply because a company's share price has fallen. In some cases, a very high yield may reflect concerns about the company's financial condition or the sustainability of its dividend.

Companies can also reduce or stop dividend payments. A dividend is not guaranteed.

What else should you look at?

Dividend yield is only one piece of the picture. Investors may also look at factors such as:

One useful companion metric is the dividend payout ratio, which shows how much of a company's earnings are being paid as dividends.

To see how the two metrics work together, read Dividend Yield vs. Payout Ratio .

  • Dividend payout ratio
  • Earnings and cash flow
  • Dividend history
  • Debt levels
  • Business stability
  • Total return

A company with a lower yield but stronger finances may be very different from a company offering an unusually high yield because its share price has fallen sharply.
